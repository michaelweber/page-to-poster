# The Brine Chapel

*A one-page location for any fantasy game. Written for this repository as an example; use it freely (CC0).*

The salt miners of Harrow Deep cut a chapel into the rock sixty years ago, so the night shift could pray without
climbing nine hundred steps. Then the brine came up. The company sealed the lower workings, the miners left, and
the chapel has stood dark ever since. Last month a shepherd found the head-house door open and fresh candle wax on
the stair.

## What is going on

Sister Odile Varn, the chapel's last lay keeper, never left. She believes the Salt Saint sleeps below and that the
brine is his breath. For years she lowered offerings into the sump. Lately she lowers travellers. Three smugglers
keep a cache in the grotto; they know she is there and stay out of her way.

## Ways in

- **The head-house stair** (area 1) is the front door.
- **The smugglers' adit** opens into the grotto (area 8) from a gully a quarter of a mile west.
- **The winch shaft** (area 7) drops to the drowned workings. Nobody comes up it.

## The chapel level

Passages are dressed stone, about three paces wide. South of the head house the miners' road reaches a three-way
junction with a signpost: north to the head house, south to the grotto, east and then south to the gallery.

1. **Head House.** Twelve paces by ten. The winding gear is rusted solid, its rope still running up through the
   roof. A stair in the west wall climbs to the surface. Crates of lamp oil are stacked in the south-east corner
   (four flasks are still good). Drips of candle wax lead east. A swollen door in the north wall opens on area 2.
2. **Tally Room.** The clerk's office. A desk, a wall of pigeonhole shelves, a row of miners' lockers, a weighing
   table. The last page of the ledger lists eleven names "given to the Saint", in a fresh hand.
3. **Nave.** The chapel proper: twenty-four paces by eighteen, vaulted eight high on eight salt-crusted pillars.
   A cross-aisle runs from the west door to the east door. North of it stand the miners' pews, facing an altar cut
   from a single block of salt. Two braziers burn beside it; someone tends them. West of the altar stands the
   Salt Saint, twice life size; east of it a second plinth is empty, its figure in pieces. The wall behind the
   altar is a slab that pivots (secret: the brazier smoke leans toward it) into area 5. A passage south leads to
   area 6.
4. **Brine Sump.** East of the nave. A square pool of bitter-cold brine, three deep, fills most of the floor. A
   plank walkway crosses it. On the north bank stands a beam pump: an hour's work lowers the pool by half and bares
   Odile's offerings on the bottom. A drain in the north wall, knee-deep in brine and wide enough to crawl, runs to
   area 5. A passage south ends at the door of area 7, barred from the far side.
5. **Ossuary.** Low and close. Stone niches of miners' bones line two walls. Of the three sarcophagi, the middle
   one stands open and has been slept in: it is Odile's bed. Her chest holds the chapel plate and the winch keys.
6. **Collapsed Gallery.** The roof came down. Two winding crawls are left between the rock falls, one from each
   northern passage; they meet in the middle. The way east toward area 7 is plugged solid.
7. **Lower Winch.** A hand winch straddles a round shaft down to the drowned workings. Barrels of pitch, a rack
   of rope. The north door is barred on this side.
8. **Crystal Grotto.** A natural cave the miners broke into. Salt crystals as long as an arm. Among them the
   smugglers' cache: crates, casks, a bedroll and a shuttered lantern. A hand-dug adit leads out to the west.

## While they explore (roll d6 every half hour)

1. A lantern crosses the far end of the passage and is gone: Odile, checking her braziers.
2. The brine breathes: every pool and puddle rises a hand's width, sighs, and falls.
3. A smuggler, on the way to or from the cache (2 in 6: all three).
4. Salt-blind rats, a dozen, drawn to lamp oil.
5. The braziers gutter. Odile will come to relight them within ten minutes.
6. Three slow knocks from below, in the winch shaft.
